## What's genuinely confirmed vs. flagged as unknown

**Confirmed, used as fact (cite, never invent — per this KB's charter):**
- Legal name, company number, registered office, contact details — as used throughout this KB's Amfa drafts.
- Warranty (up to 10 years, manufacturing defects/workmanship, correct use/maintenance), manufacturing lead times by product line, in-house delivery/installation — `Brand-and-Voice/Amfa-Furniture-Ltd.md`.
- Deposit 50%, payment by bank transfer or card, balance due before delivery, and reliance on the statutory bespoke-goods cancellation exemption — Minda, confirmed directly 2026-09-26 (this session).

**Flagged, not invented:**
1. **Quote validity (30 days) and snagging-report window (14 days)** — reasonable, commercially standard defaults, not sourced facts. Flagged here so Minda can shorten/lengthen either if she has a different preference; a solicitor reviewing the document should sanity-check both regardless.
2. **Commercial/trade orders** — the document is written for a standard consumer order. If Amfa's commercial furniture line (office/retail/hotel fit-outs) uses a different payment schedule (e.g. staged payments across a larger project), that isn't captured here and may need its own clause or a separate commercial terms document.
3. **The bespoke-goods cancellation exemption (§9)** — the underlying law (Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, reg 28(1)(b)) is real and the drafting reflects Minda's explicit choice to rely on it rather than offer a voluntary grace period. The exact point at which the exemption is safest to rely on (order confirmation + deposit vs. only once physical production starts) is a genuine legal nuance worth a solicitor's specific sign-off, given real consumer-protection exposure if worded too broadly.
