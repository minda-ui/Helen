## Findings

### 1. Case study — two editorial lines went beyond the source (fixed)

The case study's own header states "nothing added beyond what the source page states," but two sentences
didn't meet that bar:

- *"It's the same standard Fishbone Construction holds across its Fit Out work — attention to detail
  that's built to hold up, not just to photograph well."* — extends a Rolex-specific result into an
  implied claim about consistency across all Fit Out work. The Rolex page doesn't make that claim; no
  other project page was checked to substantiate it either.
- *"...the kind of names that don't take a second chance on a contractor."* — an unsourced implied claim
  about client selectivity/reputation. Reads as puffery under the charter's no-unbacked-superlative rule,
  even though no individual superlative word is used.

**Fix:** both lines removed. The case study now states only what's directly on the source page.

### 2. Social Post 3 — stat misattributed to one service line (fixed)

Original: *"Our Fit Out work alone has delivered for names like Rolex, Armani, Versace, Superdry, Peloton
and Sofa.com — 120+ projects and counting."*

The "120+ projects" figure is the company's own **overall, company-wide** public claim (from the site's
stats, also captured in `Brand-and-Voice/Fishbone-Construction-Ltd.md`'s stats table) — it is not
broken down by service line anywhere on the source site. Framing it as "Fit Out work alone... 120+
projects" implies the whole figure is Fit-Out-specific, which isn't substantiated.

**Fix:** reworded to *"Our Fit Out work has delivered for names like Rolex, Armani, Versace, Superdry,
Peloton and Sofa.com — part of 120+ projects completed across all our services,"* which keeps the client
list (verified, on the "Our Work" portfolio) but attributes the stat correctly.

### 3. Minor / not changed

- Social Post 3's Fit Out line drops the source's "such as" before "retail, commercial and leisure"
  (source: *"...biggest names across sectors **such as** retail, commercial and leisure"*), which loses a
  little of the non-exhaustive framing. Low risk — flagging for awareness, not fixed, since it doesn't
  change the substance of the claim and matches how the case study is written elsewhere.
- Punctuation-only differences from the sourced quotes (em dash vs. comma in a couple of places) — not a
  factual issue, left as natural adaptation for social-post rhythm.
- Emoji use (🏗️, ✅, numbered emojis, 🔹) is heavier than the source site's own plain prose. Construction's
  voice is described as "straightforward, confident, no-nonsense" with no explicit steer on emoji use one
  way or the other; this matches the convention already used in Amfa's launch set. Flagging as a style
  choice for Minda to weigh in on, not a compliance issue.

## What was already clean

- All quoted lines, the tagline, positioning line, standfirst, three differentiators, four service
  descriptions, both process framings, and the eight-project portfolio names all traced cleanly to
  `Brand-and-Voice/Fishbone-Construction-Ltd.md` / `HSRC-08`.
- No location, timeline, budget or client detail was invented anywhere in either draft.
- No superlatives ("best," "award-winning," "market-leading") appear.
- UK spelling consistent throughout ("organised," etc.).
- Contact details (email, phone) match the confirmed source exactly in both drafts.
